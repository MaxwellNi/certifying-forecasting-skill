# Sealed synthetic comparison on four binary laws

This is a bounded synthetic performance assessment, not a new task result or a
novelty claim. The four laws, four raw-row budgets, 500 independent repetitions,
methods and Monte Carlo summaries below are fixed before executing the run.
`binary_benchmark_seal.json` records UTC and source/configuration SHA-256 values
before sampling benchmark outcomes. This local seal identifies the executed
specification; it is not an external preregistration or proof of research priority.

## Exact support calculation and stronger same-information comparators

All methods are given the same known binary support V,W in {0,1}, one category
of probability one, and the same IID raw-row sequence per repetition. Encode
states as 2V+W, so counts are ordered 00,01,10,11. Positions with the same
observed state remain distinct raw draws.

For binary values, including ties,

    a(V_i,V_j) = [1+V_i-V_j]/2.

Expanding the complete ordered triple product, its two linear difference terms
average to zero. Its quadratic term averages to

    [n sum_i V_i W_i - (sum_i V_i)(sum_i W_i)]/[n(n-1)].

The one-category fourth term is exactly 1/4. Therefore

    U3-U4 = [n N_11 - (N_10+N_11)(N_01+N_11)]/[4n(n-1)].       (1)

This is one quarter of the unbiased sample covariance, with expectation

    theta = [p_11-(p_10+p_11)(p_01+p_11)]/4.                    (2)

`binary_counts.py` evaluates (1) as an exact Fraction. Deleting any occurrence
of state s replaces its count by N_s-1. There are at most four distinct
leave-one values; their exact multiplicity-weighted sum of squared deviations
gives the same V_J as the quadratic row algorithm. Neither center nor variance
uses simulated resampling of kernels. Time and memory are constant in raw n
once the four counts are available; reading/counting all raw rows is still
charged and takes O(n) time.

An independent oracle enumerates all 4^4=256 ordered state quadruples. For each
quadruple with multiplicities m_s, its number of distinct-position instances is
product_s (N_s)_(m_s). Multiplying this by its ordered kernel value, summing and
dividing by (n)_4 proves an independent check against the complete estimator.
The exact count formula and oracle agree on all four-row type patterns, on
random larger counts, and against every deletion from the original row code.

Binary support also makes independent references unnecessary:

    a(V_i,V_ref)-a(V_j,V_ref) = (V_i-V_j)/2.

The four-lane stratified score is thus Z=(V_i-V_j)(W_i-W_j)/8 and has range
[-1/8,1/8]. Its expectation is theta because E[(V_i-V_j)(W_i-W_j)]=2Cov(V,W).
The n/4 scores produced by the original design use the first n/2 rows as paired
focals, while all n raw rows remain charged. A stronger binary-specific
classical comparator forms n/2 disjoint pairs from **all n rows** and applies
the same bounded-mean betting rule. All paired scores are independent because
their raw positions are disjoint.

Equation (1) is also the complete order-two U statistic of this pair kernel,
whose width is 1/4. The replacement counting proof in FULL_U_VARIANCE_THEORY.md
then gives M_n<=1/(2n), J_n<=1/(n-1), improving the generic one-category
J_n<=2/(n-1). The same full-U empirical Bernstein proof applies with these
constants. Its previous-sample variance transfer remains valid (only projections
through order two are nonzero). These binary-specific constants are given
equally to the applicable comparators; they are not learned from the sample.

The direct four-row block comparator uses disjoint consecutive quadruples of
the same raw sequence and the exact symmetrized h_s lookup table. Its range
[-1/12,1/12] and target theta hold for all one-category laws. This comparator
consumes all n rows in n/4 independent blocks. Overlapping complete-U kernels
are never treated as an independent betting stream.

## Fixed cases and budgets

The supplied order-00,01,10,11 probability vectors are:

| Law | Probabilities | Exact theta | Purpose |
|---|---|---:|---|
| balanced_independent | (1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4) | 0 | nondegenerate null |
| balanced_anticorrelated | (0,1/2,1/2,0) | -1/16 | negative target |
| balanced_perfectly_correlated | (1/2,0,0,1/2) | 1/16 | exactly zero first projection |
| rare_perfectly_correlated | (99/100,0,0,1/100) | 99/40000 | rare, weak signal |

Budgets are 512, 2048, 8192 and 32768 IID raw observations, each divisible by
four. There are 500 repetitions per law/budget, generated by independent
SeedSequence([2026091331,law_index,budget_index,repetition]) streams. Methods
within each repetition see identical rows. Different budgets are independent,
not selected stopping times or nested optional sampling. No real dataset is read.

## Methods fixed before execution

- Generic full-U Hoeffding, empirical Bernstein, and jointly allocated maximum.
- Binary-specific full-U empirical Bernstein and its joint maximum with the
  valid generic full-U Hoeffding bound.
- Original four-lane stratified Hoeffding and H/empirical-Bernstein hybrid.
- Binary-range specializations of those same two four-lane intervals, replacing
  the generic score width one by its supplied binary width 1/4.
- Original one-cell profiled betting with its generic range [-1/2,1/2].
- Binary-range four-lane pair betting on the same n/4 scores.
- Direct four-row h_s betting on n/4 independent blocks.
- Binary-specific all-raw pair betting on n/2 independent scores.

Every primary betting comparator uses the same fixed 64 fractions
geomspace(1e-4,.99,64) with uniform mixture weights. The fractions are fixed
represented numeric constants and included in the seal. No best single bet
is chosen after seeing scores. For independent scores Z>=l with l<0 and null
E Z<=0, each factor 1+f Z/(-l) is nonnegative and has expectation at most one.
Its product and the fixed mixture therefore have expectation at most one.
The fixed-sample p value min(1,1/e_mix) is valid by Markov's inequality. The
one-cell profile has its infimum at shifted mean 1/2, giving precisely factors
1+2f Z; the binary-tight score bound gives factors 1+8f Z; direct h_s blocks
give factors 1+12f h_s. This is classical bounded-mean betting, not new theory.

All decisions use raw per-method alpha=.05. These separate-method performance
comparisons do not license choosing an unadjusted test after seeing the results.
The two components of each hybrid/joint interval receive alpha/2 internally.

## Output and paired Monte Carlo uncertainty

Save all per-repetition rejection indicators and interval lower bounds, where
an interval exists, together with true theta and charged raw counts. Report
rejection counts/rates, target-lower-bound failures, mean centers and computation
time by method, law and budget. Under theta<=0, rejection is a false certificate;
under theta>0 it is detection of this fixed synthetic target.

For every pair of methods within a law/budget, use paired differences D_r of
their rejection indicators. D_r lies in [-1,1], and repetitions are independent.
Report the mean difference, discordant counts, and a pointwise 95% empirical
Bernstein interval, intersected with [-1,1]:

    mean(D) +/- [sqrt(2 S_D^2 log(80)/500)
                  + 14 log(80)/(3*499)].

Each one-sided Maurer-Pontil bounded-mean interval receives .025, and its
log(2/.025)=log(80) and range two give the displayed formula. This is a valid
paired Monte Carlo interval for the rejection-probability difference, not a
confidence interval for theta. It is pointwise across the many method pairs;
no simultaneous Monte Carlo superiority claim is made. Report all planned
pairs, cases and budgets, including ties and failures.

These supplied laws probe arithmetic, degeneracy and weak signals on a tiny
support. They do not span arbitrary category imbalance or real forecasting
structure and cannot establish universal dominance. In particular, a method
that knows the binary support can exploit identities absent in a generic law.
